Mistakes & Loser Management

Winning trades take care of themselves. Accounts are lost on the other side — oversized positions, unmanaged losers, and held-to-expiration tails. This page is the defense manual.

Oversizing Is the #1 Killer

Traders who blow up on undefined-risk options rarely fail because of the strategy. They fail because the position was too big for the tail that arrived. Risk-of-ruin research tells the story plainly: cutting position size can drop the probability of blowing up from roughly 13% to under 1%. Same strategy, same market — only the size changed.

3–7% per trade

Undefined-risk trades get 3–7% of net liquidation value each. Defined-risk trades get 1–3%. These are ceilings, not suggestions.

15% per underlying

No single ticker — no matter how "safe" — gets more than 15% of net liq. Concentration turns a normal loss into a career event.

Stay consistent

A 10% position next to a 1% position breaks the statistical model the whole system rests on. Uniform small size is what lets the law of large numbers work.

Managing Losers: Roll, Don't Hope

For undefined-risk positions, the canonical defense is rolling — not stop-losses.

Roll the untested side

When one side of a strangle is breached, roll the non-losing side closer to the stock price. This collects fresh premium and cuts the position's deltas (a common rule of thumb: reduce deltas by 30–50%). If the underlying keeps running, roll the entire position out in time — extend duration rather than accept the loss at the worst moment.

Time exits beat P/L stops

The popular "close when the loss doubles" stop feels disciplined, but research on short-premium portfolios found P/L-based stops underperform the simple 21-DTE time exit. Stops tend to lock in losses on positions that would have recovered; the time exit cuts gamma risk while letting mean reversion do its work. For defined-risk trades, don't manage losers at all — the wings cap the damage; just exit at 21 DTE.

The Classic Beginner Mistakes

❌ Trading without a mechanical plan

Entering a trade with no pre-set profit target, exit date, or defense — then improvising under stress. Every entry on this site comes with its management attached. No plan, no trade.

❌ Holding through earnings

Most earnings moves land inside the expected move, which tempts sellers to stay. The tail is brutally asymmetric: one gap wipes out months of collected premium. Stay small or step aside.

❌ Holding short premium to expiration

Gamma risk accelerates in the final weeks. The last few dollars of premium are the most expensive dollars you'll ever try to collect. Manage at 50%, exit at 21 DTE.

❌ Selling when IV is low

Low IV means thin premium for the same risk — and never pay record prices to buy volatility either. When vol is cheap, sell further out, size down, or wait.

❌ Trading illiquid underlyings

Wide bid/ask spreads are a hidden tax on every entry, exit, and roll. If you can't get in and out cheaply, the edge is gone before the trade starts.

❌ Treating premium like free money

Premium selling is not income without risk — it's risk with income. Respect the tail: size for margin expansion and gap risk, because history's worst days (the 2018 vol spike, March 2020, the August 2024 spike) are always one headline away.

The bottom line: undefined risk does not mean unmanaged risk. Small size, the 21-DTE exit, rolling instead of hoping, and staying out of binary events — that's the entire survival kit. Everything else is optimization.
